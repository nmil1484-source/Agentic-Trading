# Reward-to-Risk Requirements and Position Sizing for Low-Win-Rate Trend-Following/Momentum Crypto Strategies

## What is the mathematical relationship between win rate, reward-to-risk ratio, and expectancy/profit factor, and what breakeven/profitable R:R thresholds do practitioners cite for win rates around 15%, 20%, and 30%?

### Takeaway
Breakeven win rate is a clean, undisputed formula — `1 / (1 + R)` where R is the reward-to-risk ratio — and expectancy in R-multiples is `(WinRate × AvgWin) − (LossRate × AvgLoss)`. At a 15-30% win rate, the breakeven R:R is steep: roughly 2.3:1 to 5.7:1 just to avoid losing money before costs, and materially higher once fees/slippage/funding are added — meaningfully above the 1.3:1–1.5:1 floor currently used for crypto in this system's rules.

### Cited Findings
- Expectancy (in R-multiples) = (Win Rate × Average Win) − (Loss Rate × Average Loss), with Loss Rate = 1 − Win Rate; e.g. a 40% win rate with 2.5R average winners gives 0.40×2.5 − 0.60×1 = +0.40R per trade — [NexusFi: Expectancy](https://nexusfi.com/a/risk-management/expectancy)
- Breakeven win rate = 1 / (1 + R): at R:R = 1.5:1 → 40% breakeven; 2:1 → 33.3%; 3:1 → 25%; 4:1 → 20% — [TradingView: Winrates required to breakeven relative to stop/target sizes](https://vn.tradingview.com/chart/ZC1%21/aJvJwYQK-Winrates-required-to-breakeven-relative-to-stop-target-sizes); confirmed by [NexusFi: Risk/Reward Ratio](https://nexusfi.com/a/risk-management/risk-reward-ratio)
- Applying the same formula to the target win-rate band: breakeven R:R at 30% win rate ≈ 2.33:1; at 20% win rate = 4:1; at 15% win rate ≈ 5.67:1 (derived directly from `R = (1 − W)/W`, not independently stated by a single source, but a direct algebraic restatement of the breakeven-win-rate formula above)
- A profitability example contrasting win-rate illusions: a 40% win rate with $600 avg winners / $200 avg losers nets +$120/trade (strongly profitable), while a 70% win rate with $150 winners / $350 losers nets $0/trade (breakeven despite a much higher hit rate) — [CrossTrade: Win Rate vs Expectancy](https://crosstrade.io/learn/performance-metrics/win-rate-vs-expectancy)
- Costs (commissions, exchange fees, funding/taxes) raise the true breakeven win rate above the frictionless formula's output — [NexusFi: Risk/Reward Ratio](https://nexusfi.com/a/risk-management/risk-reward-ratio)
- Higher R:R targets are typically harder to hit: a distant profit target is reached less often than a nearby one, so a 1:5 setup usually has a lower win rate than a 1:1 setup — same source as above, a structural tradeoff rather than a free lunch
- Sample-size caveat on any measured win rate/expectancy: one source recommends at least 100, ideally 200+ closed trades before trusting an expectancy figure, warning that under ~50 trades the figure is dominated by randomness — [NexusFi: Expectancy](https://nexusfi.com/a/risk-management/expectancy) (secondary/forum source, included because it bears directly on how much confidence to place in this account's own ~12-trade crypto sample)
- Real-world illustration of a hidden bad payoff ratio: a live bot with a 78% win rate was flat over six years because its reward:risk was only 1:0.34 — [dev.to: We graded 13 famous trading strategies on 6 years of data](https://dev.to/tessen/we-graded-13-famous-trading-strategies-on-6-years-of-data-all-13-failed-26o4)
- A Reddit practitioner argued a 2:1 payoff at a 35% win rate is "most likely not profitable, at best breaks even" once real-world frictions are included — same dev.to-linked discussion thread (secondary/forum source, directionally consistent with the breakeven-formula math at that win rate: 2:1 breakeven win rate is 33.3%, so 35% is only marginally above breakeven before costs)

### Inferences
- At this system's current §21 crypto reward-to-risk floor of 1.3:1 (breakeven win rate ≈ 43.5%), a strategy actually running a 15-30% win rate is far below breakeven by the standard formula — it would need roughly 2.3:1 to 5.7:1 just to reach the breakeven line, and higher still after fees/funding/slippage are subtracted from both sides of every trade.
- The practical implication for a crypto system with a measured ~16.7% win rate (per this account's own tracked history, see Gaps) is that its reward-to-risk floor is the single most important lever — more important than confirmation count or stop-distance ceiling — and it should be benchmarked against the ~5.7:1 breakeven line for a 15% win rate, not a flat 1.3-1.5:1 figure borrowed from equity swing trading's much higher (~40-55%+) win-rate regime.

### Gaps
- No single primary/authoritative source gives a breakeven table explicitly annotated for 15%, 20%, and 30% win rates together — the 15%/20%/30% figures above are my own direct application of the universally-cited `1/(1+R)` formula, not independently published at those exact points. Flagging this as a derived calculation, not a separately-sourced claim.
- No source quantifies exactly how much higher the breakeven threshold needs to be after typical crypto trading costs (spread, taker fees, funding on any leveraged variant, slippage on resting stops) — directionally confirmed as "higher," not numerically specified anywhere found.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences

## What does the Kelly criterion formula say about optimal bet sizing for a ~15-30% win-rate strategy at a given R:R, and why do practitioners use fractional Kelly?

### Takeaway
The Kelly formula `f* = W − (1 − W)/R` (equivalently `f* = (b·p − q)/b`) returns a negative (no-bet) answer for most realistic 15-30%-win-rate/R:R combinations unless R is quite large (breakeven R at these win rates is the same 2.3:1-5.7:1 band from the first section); where it is positive, the Kelly fraction at low win rates is small (single-digit percent of equity), and practitioners near-universally cut it to a half or quarter of the computed value because full Kelly's growth-maximizing property comes with severe, empirically-cited drawdowns (50-80%) and extreme sensitivity to estimation error in W and R — both of which are worse for a 15-30%-win-rate system than a 50%+ one, because the win-rate estimate itself is statistically noisier at low sample counts and low hit rates.

### Cited Findings
- Formula, both forms given consistently across sources: `f* = W − (1 − W)/R` where W = win rate, R = average win ÷ average loss (payoff ratio); equivalently `f* = (b·p − q)/b` where b = net odds (≈ R), p = win rate, q = 1 − p — [LuxAlgo: Kelly Criterion](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/kelly-criterion.md); [TradesViz: Kelly Criterion glossary](https://www.tradesviz.com/glossary/kelly-criterion/)
- Worked example at a 55% win rate, 1.5 payoff ratio: full Kelly = 0.55 − 0.45/1.5 = 0.55 − 0.30 = 25% of equity per trade; half-Kelly = 12.5%; quarter-Kelly = 6.25% — [Kelly Criterion Position Sizing Guide](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/kelly-criterion-position-sizing-guide)
- My own direct application of the same formula to the 15-30%-win-rate band relevant to this system's crypto strategy (not independently published at these exact points, but a straightforward restatement of the cited formula):
  - **20% win rate, R = 4 (exactly breakeven):** f* = 0.20 − 0.80/4 = 0. No position justified below R = 4.
  - **20% win rate, R = 5:** f* = 0.20 − 0.80/5 = 0.20 − 0.16 = 0.04 → full Kelly = 4% of equity; half-Kelly = 2%; quarter-Kelly = 1%.
  - **20% win rate, R = 6:** f* = 0.20 − 0.80/6 ≈ 0.20 − 0.133 = 0.067 → full Kelly ≈ 6.7%; half-Kelly ≈ 3.3%.
  - **30% win rate, R = 2.33 (exactly breakeven):** f* = 0 by definition of breakeven R.
  - **30% win rate, R = 3:** f* = 0.30 − 0.70/3 = 0.30 − 0.233 = 0.067 → full Kelly ≈ 6.7%; half-Kelly ≈ 3.3%.
  - **30% win rate, R = 4:** f* = 0.30 − 0.70/4 = 0.30 − 0.175 = 0.125 → full Kelly = 12.5%; half-Kelly ≈ 6.25%.
  (derivation method itself sourced to the LuxAlgo/StockAlarm formula above; the specific 15-30%-win-rate numeric plug-ins are this researcher's own arithmetic)
- A directly-relevant practitioner example in the sources: "a trend-following strategy wins only 40% of the time but its average win is twice its average loss" → f* = 0.10 (10% full Kelly), explicitly illustrating that a win rate well below 50% is fine under Kelly logic *if* the payoff ratio is large enough — [search synthesis citing multiple Kelly-criterion explainer pages, incl. LuxAlgo/StockAlarm/TradesViz above]
- Why fractional Kelly: "the growth penalty for betting above the true optimum is severe while the penalty for betting below it is mild" — asymmetric cost of error is the stated rationale for under-betting relative to the computed optimum — [LuxAlgo: Kelly Criterion](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/kelly-criterion.md)
- Approximate drawdown figures by Kelly fraction (one source, explicitly flagged by that source as a rough estimate): full Kelly 50-80% max drawdown; half-Kelly 25-40%; quarter-Kelly 12-20% — [Kelly Criterion Position Sizing Guide](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/kelly-criterion-position-sizing-guide)
- Half-Kelly's commonly-cited tradeoff: "approximately 75% of the [theoretical growth] rate with roughly half the maximum drawdown" of full Kelly — [glossary source via search synthesis, see Kelly criterion glossary pages above]
- Estimation-error rationale: a 55% win rate measured from only 50 trades could realistically be anywhere from ~48% to ~62% due to sampling noise, so overestimating the true edge from a small sample leads directly to oversizing if full Kelly is used on the point estimate — [search synthesis of Kelly criterion explainer pages above]
- Quarter-Kelly specifically recommended by at least one source when backtests involve fewer than ~150 trades — [search synthesis, Kelly criterion worked-example pages above] (this directly bears on the ~12-trade crypto sample in this account's own records, see Gaps)
- Negative-edge check: if the formula returns zero or negative (e.g., 45% win rate at 1:1 payoff → 0.45 − 0.55 = −0.10), the strategy has no mathematical edge at that R:R and Kelly logic says not to bet any fraction of capital, regardless of how the position-sizing rule is otherwise written — [search synthesis of Kelly criterion explainer pages above]
- Kelly/optimal-f both assume the win-rate and payoff-ratio inputs are accurate; Ralph Vince's "optimal f" generalizes Kelly to variable-size wins/losses (rather than Kelly's binary same-size-outcome assumption) by optimizing terminal wealth (TWR) directly over the historical trade sequence — [MultiCharts: The Foundations of Money Management III](https://www.multicharts.com/support/base/the-foundations-of-money-management-iii/); [Quantpedia: Beware of Excessive Leverage – Introduction to Kelly and Optimal F](https://quantpedia.com/beware-of-excessive-leverage-introduction-to-kelly-and-optimal-f/)
- Optimal-f drawdown property (MultiCharts, citing Vince): "the maximal drawdown with optimal f value will be at least fopt% of account" — e.g. an optimal f of 0.5 implies drawdowns of at least 50% are to be expected, even though f* is "optimal" for long-run compound growth — [MultiCharts: Foundations of Money Management III](https://www.multicharts.com/support/base/the-foundations-of-money-management-iii/)
- Vince himself is reported to generally recommend "partial Kelly" (a fraction of the full Kelly/optimal-f bet) specifically to reduce realized drawdowns — [secondary PDF summary of Vince's "Portfolio Management Formulas"] (low-confidence source — multiple identical PDF mirrors with no clearly identifiable original publisher found; treat as directionally consistent with the higher-confidence fractional-Kelly sources above rather than as independent confirmation)
- Fat-tail warning specific to volatile/crypto-like assets: standard Kelly assumes a roughly normal/thin-tailed return distribution; when a strategy's real distribution is fat-tailed (a few large winners, many small losers — explicitly the shape expected for crypto trend-following), standard Kelly can overstate a "safe" position size because it underestimates tail-loss risk — [search synthesis of a trading-skill Kelly-criterion guide] (practitioner-guide source, not peer-reviewed, but the underlying statistical point — Kelly's classical derivation assumes a known, stable bet with fixed odds, and fat tails/regime shifts violate that assumption — is standard and uncontroversial in quantitative finance)
- One practitioner guide's concrete crypto-adjacent convention: cap Kelly-derived sizing at 0.1-0.25× full Kelly for highly volatile/speculative tokens, hard-cap absolute position size at 2-5% of portfolio regardless of the Kelly formula's output, and treat any full-Kelly output above ~30% as a signal that the win-rate/payoff inputs are themselves wrong rather than a true trading opportunity — [search synthesis of a Kelly-criterion trading-skill guide for meme tokens] (lower-confidence, single-source practitioner convention, not an academic or widely-corroborated figure — flagged as such)

### Inferences
- Applying this system's own measured crypto profile (≈16.7% win rate per the Gaps note below) to the Kelly formula: breakeven R is ≈5.0:1 (`(1−0.167)/0.167`), meaning at the system's current 1.3:1 crypto R:R floor, Kelly would return a strongly negative fraction — i.e., the formula says this edge, as currently measured, should not be sized at all, let alone sized up. This is independent confirmation, via a different mathematical lens, of the breakeven-win-rate finding in section 1.
- Even in the regime where this system's current risk-per-trade sizing (1.5% of equity, §21 item 4) happens to be positive-Kelly-compatible, the worked examples above show full-Kelly fractions at realistic 20-30% win rates and 3-6:1 payoffs cluster in the 4-13% range — meaning a fixed 1.5%/trade risk budget is already conservative relative to full Kelly (roughly quarter- to tenth-Kelly) *if* the payoff ratio were actually in the 4-6:1 range. The binding constraint in this system is not over-betting relative to Kelly; it is that the reward-to-risk floor (1.3:1) is far below the level needed for Kelly (or even simple breakeven arithmetic) to justify betting any fraction at all.
- Given the explicit practitioner caution about quarter-Kelly for sub-150-trade backtests and this account's own small live sample, even a theoretically-positive Kelly calculation here would warrant the more conservative end of the fractional-Kelly spectrum (quarter-Kelly or lower), not half-Kelly.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative, named academic paper (vs. blog/glossary restatement) deriving the exact Kelly formula was retrieved in this pass; the formula itself is well-established and uncontroversial in the literature (traceable to Kelly's original 1956 Bell Labs paper, cited by name in multiple sources), but this research pass relied on secondary explainer pages rather than the primary 1956 paper or a peer-reviewed finance-journal treatment.
- The "50-80% / 25-40% / 12-20%" drawdown-by-Kelly-fraction figures and the Vince "partial Kelly" recommendation both came from sources explicitly flagged (by the search process itself, and by me) as rough/unofficial — a stronger primary source (e.g., Vince's own *The Mathematics of Money Management* or *Portfolio Management Formulas*) should be consulted if precise drawdown figures are needed for a final report.

## Is there specific literature or practitioner commentary on reward-to-risk/win-rate profiles for crypto trend-following specifically?

### Takeaway
Crypto-specific win-rate/R:R data exists but is thin, scattered across single-author preprints, vendor blogs, and backtest screenshots rather than consolidated practitioner literature; the figures found (40-60% win rates with 2-4× payoff ratios in several crypto-specific backtests) are generally *higher* win rate than this account's own realized ~16.7%, suggesting either this account's specific execution/gate design is unusually weak, or its sample is too small/idiosyncratic to compare against these backtest figures.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 single-author academic preprint on crypto momentum found time-series momentum earned ~32% annualized return with better risk-adjusted performance than cross-sectional momentum (which had max drawdowns of 55%), across 8 major cryptocurrencies, 2020-October 2025 — no win rate or R:R given in the retrieved summary — [academic study via search synthesis, 2025 cryptocurrency momentum comparative study](https://www.zurnalai.vu.lt/BATP/article/view/44540)
- A separate 2026 arXiv preprint ("AdaptiveTrend": Bui & Nguyen, single-author/two-author, not peer-reviewed) reports an annualized Sharpe of 2.41, max drawdown −12.7%, Calmar 3.18, across 150+ crypto pairs, 36 months out-of-sample (2022-2024), using 6-hour bars and a dynamic ATR-calibrated trailing stop — abstract did not report win rate, reward-to-risk, profit factor, or a Kelly/fixed-fractional sizing rule; full paper body was not retrievable as readable text in this pass — [arXiv:2602.11708, "Systematic Trend-Following with Adaptive Portfolio Construction"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11708)
- A vendor analysis (Arch Intelligence) of a Web3-token momentum index reported a 55% monthly win rate at a 14-day rebalance period, with the edge shrinking as the rebalancing period lengthened — vendor blog, not independently verified — [Arch Finance: Momentum investing strategy and the crypto market](https://blog.arch.finance/momentum-investing-strategy-and-the-crypto-market)

### Inferences
- Every crypto-specific win-rate figure actually found in this research pass (47-60%) sits well above both this document's assumed 15-30% band and this account's own realized ~16.7% win rate — none of the retrieved crypto backtests actually validate a 15-30%-win-rate regime as the "normal" expected shape for crypto trend-following.
